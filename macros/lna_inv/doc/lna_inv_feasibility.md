## What the numbers say

**Simpler, and 2 dB short: 4.36 dB at 3.6 mA, against the common source's 2.29 dB at 2.0 mA on the same shared pad.**
The pair carries 54 % of the output noise, the feedback resistor 9 %, the shunt coil 1 %, and the sweep in [`lna_inv_sweep.md`](lna_inv_sweep.md) shows the floor: 5.8 dB at 2.5 mA, 4.4 at 4, 4.0 at 6, 4.1 at 8, so current buys nothing past 6 mA and the 2.5 dB row is out of reach at any current.
The estimate, 1 + RS/Rf + gamma/(gm RS), reads 1.6 dB at 4 mA and is wrong by the same 2.7 dB at every point, which is the PSP RF model's gate and substrate network on 150 and 300 um of device and the noise gain of a shunt-feedback stage that the estimate does not carry. The bench is the number.

**What it is good at.** S11 −28 dB with nothing tuned, IIP3 +5 dBm, P1dB −9 dBm, no coil on chip and no gate coil off chip, only the shunt coil the shared pad needs anyway. The match depends on the load: the sweep solves the feedback for the bench's 200 ohm and for 100 ohm, and the real mixer sits between.

**When it would be enough.** At the receiver this LNA is 4.9 dB behind the tuned common source, -81.9 dBm of LE 1M sensitivity against -86.8, and the gap is selectivity rather than noise figure: with nothing tuned it rejects 8.2 dB at 5 GHz off band against 51.4, so the Section 4.3 blocker between 30 MHz and 2 GHz becomes the binding test and the converter's input full scale has to sit about 6 dB higher. The chain being converter-dominated is the other half of it: the 2.0 dB of device noise figure that separates this LNA from a tuned common gate is worth 0.4 dB of sensitivity, so what a coil-free candidate has to buy elsewhere is out-of-band rejection and not noise.
No selectivity either, which the tuned LNA also lacked until the shunt-series match around the shared pad gave it some; the inverter behind the same pad network gets the same 2nd-order shape from the pad and nothing from itself.


## Method and limits

- Sizing: `scripts/lna_inv_sizing.py` on the gm/Id tables, the pair at its own trip point, VGS = VDS = 0.6 V, so the current sets both widths and the feedback resistor is solved for 50 ohm against the load. The match depends on the load, and the bench's 200 ohm is a stand-in for the mixer.
- Small-signal (`lna_inv_tb_sp`) runs the PSP RF model, `rfmode=1`. Harmonic balance (`lna_inv_tb_hb`) runs `rfmode=0`. The chain (`lna_inv_tb_chain`) is transient with the RF model.
- The bench's 200 ohm load stands in for the mixer and the converter, and its thermal noise is excluded from the NF row.
- Corners: the 45-point CACE set in [`lna_inv_corners.md`](lna_inv_corners.md), ngspice, small signal only. The numbers above are TT, 27 C, and the supply current alone spans 0.32 to 12.53 mA across that set, so read the corner doc before quoting any row here as the block's behaviour.
- Part and parasitic spread: [`lna_inv_robust.md`](lna_inv_robust.md), the nominal design with one thing moved at a time.
- IIP3 and P1dB are TT only. They need harmonic balance, which the CACE flow does not run.
- Not covered: layout parasitics, the pad itself beyond the shared-pad capacitance, the LO buffers, the mixer noise.
